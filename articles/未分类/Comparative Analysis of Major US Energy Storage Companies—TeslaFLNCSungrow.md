1. What is the competitive landscape of the US energy storage market, and what are the respective market shares of the main players?

The competitive landscape of the US large-scale energy storage market is relatively clear. The main players in the current US energy storage market include Tesla, Sungrow, and Fluence. Among them, Tesla holds the highest market share, at approximately 26% to 28%, approaching 30%. Sungrow and Fluence alternate between the second and third positions, with Sungrow's market share at around 17% to 18% and Fluence at about 13% to 14%. The combined market share of these three leading companies has reached roughly 60%. Other participants include BYD, whose market share is gradually increasing and has entered the top five in the US market. In addition, Samsung SDI and Schneider are also involved in market competition. Canadian Solar ranks approximately sixth to seventh in the US market.

2. How would you evaluate the competitiveness of major players such as Tesla, Sungrow, and Fluence?

Specifically, Tesla holds a market share of over 20% in the large-scale (B-end) energy storage market. Its technology does not have a significant lead. In terms of battery cells, although Tesla possesses technology, it has not established large-scale production and mainly relies on external procurement, with suppliers including CATL, LG Chem, Samsung, and Panasonic. For the lithium iron phosphate technology route commonly adopted in energy storage, Chinese companies have an absolute advantage.

Regarding inverters, Tesla's technology does not rank among the global top five. Its core advantages are threefold: first, as a domestic brand, it enjoys inherent advantages; second, it is highly familiar with the technical structure and access rules of the domestic grid; third, its founder Elon Musk's industrial layout and close ties with US capital markets. These factors enable Tesla's products to maintain a considerable market share even when priced higher than competitors.

Sungrow's core strengths lie in its PCS technology and grid-side access capabilities. Its PCS technology is in the global first tier alongside Huawei. Particularly in 35kV high-voltage direct connection technology, Sungrow is at the global forefront. Sungrow entered the US market early, collaborating with Samsung since 2013-2014, and has established its brand image through investments in numerous energy storage and power station projects. In the European and American markets, Sungrow has earned high customer recognition through long-term project experience and brand reputation. In terms of system integration, its battery cells are mainly sourced from domestic manufacturers such as First New Energy. Benefiting from the advantages of the Chinese supply chain, Sungrow's product costs are about 10-15% lower than Tesla's, giving it a clear price advantage.

3. Please compare and analyze the differences among Tesla, Fluence, and Sungrow in terms of technical capabilities and local production capacity in the long-duration energy storage market.

In the long-duration energy storage market, Fluence benefits from policy incentives aligned with California and New York's energy storage targets, allowing it to receive higher project subsidies. The company has deep collaborations with major ISOs and ITOs and participates in the formulation of grid rules, giving it an advantage in technical standards. Its business model excels in asset optimization, enhancing customer stickiness by providing power market trading support. Fluence's use of Stark modules reduces costs, and its focus on large-scale grid energy storage delivers significant economies of scale.

Sungrow's core competitiveness lies in cost and product strength, with strong inverter and system integration capabilities. However, there are policy constraints, such as the FEOC rule, which may prevent its core components from enjoying the full IRA subsidy. In addition, its local production capacity is relatively low, supply chain response is slower, and its software ecosystem for high-voltage rapid response required by AIDC is still lacking. Tesla leads in technical capability and continues to expand its advantage. Fluence has slightly improved its response efficiency by adopting SmartStark and third-party software integration. In comparison, Sungrow is at a disadvantage in terms of technical capability.

In terms of local production capacity planning, by 2028, Tesla's compliant capacity in North America is expected to reach 75 GWh. Fluence is projected to reach 18 GWh of compliant capacity by then.

Regarding delivery cycles, Tesla leads with 6-8 weeks, while Fluence and Sungrow require 10-12 weeks.

4. How would you evaluate Tesla, Fluence, and Sungrow in terms of product competitiveness, customer satisfaction, reputation, stability, and economics?

From the perspectives of overall economics, reputation, and cost, Sungrow performs best. Its advantages include lower prices and the backing of China's robust new energy industry chain, making its cost control capabilities world-leading. Technically, Sungrow's grid-side interconnection technology is highly stable, and there have been no fire or explosion incidents overseas in the past three to four years, which is attributable not only to battery cell quality but also to its outstanding system integration and grid connection capabilities.

Tesla has the strongest brand power but also the highest prices. Its market share in the B-end and G-end has not reached the over-50% level seen in the C-end, partly because Musk's political involvement has led to resistance in certain regions. Tesla's core advantage lies in its vertically integrated technology enterprise structure, especially in specific application scenarios such as AIDC, where its solutions are superior to Sungrow's because it is itself a generator of AI and computing power demand. However, its products have experienced fire and explosion incidents in regions such as Australia.

Fluence's pricing is similar to or slightly higher than Sungrow's. Its main advantage is being a domestic brand with US capital backing, making it a resource- and capital-driven company. In terms of product reputation, it may not match Tesla.

5. Is Tesla currently leading in the AI application of energy storage systems?

Tesla is currently in a leading position in the AI application of energy storage systems. The most mature solutions in the industry are mainly from Tesla and Enphase, but Enphase focuses on residential energy storage and has little involvement in large-scale energy storage. It is expected that by 2026, the industry as a whole will widely adopt such AI systems.

For the AIDC market, Tesla's products are fully compatible. Fluence can achieve partial compatibility, mainly focusing on behind-the-meter projects. At present, Sungrow can only achieve pilot compatibility, mainly targeting the edge data center market.

6. What is the outlook for Tesla's market share in the US market?

Regarding market share, Tesla currently holds about 35% in the US market and may reach even higher in the future. However, in the European and Australian markets, its share is unlikely to reach such high levels.

7. What are the specific competitive barriers for Fluence in terms of product technology and customer network?

Fluence's core competitive barriers lie mainly in long-duration energy storage technology, product modular innovation, and a strong customer network. In long-duration energy storage technology, its Gridstack Pro series can achieve 8-hour storage duration, offering a leading edge and strong grid compatibility, making it one of the preferred solutions for grid operators such as CAISO and PJM. In terms of product innovation, Fluence has launched the Smart Stack modular design, which adopts a 'battery-on-top, electronics-below' stacking structure. This design allows a single unit capacity of up to 7.5 MWh, effectively reducing transportation and installation costs and shortening deployment time. The system also offers high stability and safety, meets grid-level standards, and has been validated for long-term operation.

In terms of customer network, Fluence has a broad and stable customer base in North America, covering grid companies, municipal utilities, and long-duration energy storage project developers, with a high customer repurchase rate. Fluence has over 150 GWh of project experience globally and is a benchmark supplier in North American long-duration energy storage. Its comprehensive service and O&M system, along with a high proportion of long-term O&M contracts, ensures performance stability. In addition, Fluence has a complete local supply chain in the US, with growing capacity in Arizona, fully compliant with FEOC requirements and eligible for full IRA subsidies.

8. What are Fluence's core competitive advantages, and how will it compete with Tesla in the AIDC energy storage market?

Fluence's core advantage lies in its strategic goal of transitioning from a utility-dedicated supplier to an all-round supplier. It has established deep ties with grid and new energy developers, especially building differentiated moats in long-duration energy storage, enabling it to quickly secure large orders. In the AIDC market, Fluence's current strategy is to first enter the mid- and low-end and behind-the-meter data center energy storage markets. In the future, it plans to enhance business stability by shortening delivery cycles and expanding capacity, transforming itself from a project integrator to a product and service provider. Although it currently faces capacity shortages and long delivery cycles, its integrated software such as SmartStack has significantly improved response efficiency, and its growth potential should not be underestimated.

9. Given the volatility of US tariff policies, why are companies like Fluence or Tesla considered to have relative advantages in supply chain management?

Companies like Fluence and Tesla are considered to have advantages in coping with tariff fluctuations primarily due to supply chain certainty. For example, battery cells purchased by Fluence from AESC are produced domestically in the US, so there are no tariff fluctuation factors and costs are relatively certain. Investors and suppliers prefer to avoid uncertainty risks, and choosing companies with controllable supply chains can effectively reduce cost risks arising from policy changes.

10. How does Fluence perform in terms of compliance and data center business in the US market?

In terms of current US market pricing, Fluence does not have an advantage over Sungrow, but its compliance rate has reached 100%. If relevant legislation is enacted, Fluence will take the lead in compliance, while Sungrow will be restricted. In the AIDC field, Sungrow is relatively stronger, as Fluence can only achieve partial compatibility and mainly focuses on some large-scale projects. Sungrow faces certain difficulties entering the AIDC market, so its business focus may shift more toward the edge data center market.

11. What are Fluence's business model and market barriers in the long-duration energy storage field?

Fluence has a longer history of technical development in long-duration energy storage, while Tesla has not previously focused on this field and has concentrated on lithium battery technology. Long-duration energy storage (6-8 hours) mainly uses sodium and flow battery technologies, with the main demand coming from the grid due to stronger grid compatibility. Fluence maintains good relationships with grid operators and holds numerous patents in system integration, forming its market barriers. In the future, Fluence may also improve its profitability through software development for energy trading.

12. With Fluence's gross margin at only 3% in FY2025 and guidance of just 11-13% for FY2026, why is there still an expectation for margin improvement?

Although Fluence's gross margin is low in FY2025 and disclosed project margins are generally between 10-15%, there is still an expectation for future margin improvement. This expectation is not based on the assumption that long-duration energy storage business margins will necessarily be higher than traditional projects, but rather on the belief that Fluence will enhance its overall profitability by optimizing its systems, leveraging long-duration energy storage patents, and improving software trading capabilities as a grid service provider.

13. Can Fluence's battery supply chain support its expansion, and who are the main suppliers?

Fluence's battery supply chain is not expected to be a bottleneck for its development and can meet its market expansion needs. Its main battery cell suppliers include Envision Energy, which has a capacity of 7 GWh. The supply chain may also include Blue Planet Energy, SK On, and Ford. Ford plans to convert 20 GWh of power battery capacity to energy storage batteries, which may also be supplied externally.

14. What is the cooperation model between Fluence and Siemens, especially in the European market? Has Siemens provided key support?

The cooperation between Fluence and Siemens is not a system integration model; Siemens mainly acts as a supplier, providing PCS and some system components, engaging in technical cooperation, and holding about 28% of Fluence's shares. At the business level, Siemens has not provided direct assistance to Fluence. Fluence's orders in the European market, including large-scale energy storage projects, are entirely secured through its own design, bidding capabilities, and brand reputation. Siemens' role for Fluence is limited to supplying PCS and other system components (such as part of the BMS), but not battery cells. Therefore, Fluence has strong independent order acquisition capabilities in the European market and does not rely on Siemens.

15. From a technical perspective, what are the respective strengths and weaknesses of Sungrow and Fluence in data center energy storage applications? Is Sungrow taking steps to address its shortcomings?

An energy storage system typically consists of three core components: PCS, BMS, and EMS. Sungrow's strengths are in PCS and BMS, but its EMS lags significantly behind Fluence. Data center energy storage projects have very high requirements for EMS due to complex operating conditions. Overall, Fluence is technically more competitive. However, Sungrow is taking action, planning to integrate relevant AI technologies into its EMS system to achieve intelligent dispatching through embedded AI. This requires corresponding changes and upgrades to the control chip algorithms of its PCS and BMS to ensure that information from PCS and BMS can be effectively fed back to the EMS, which then integrates and hands over to AI for dispatch.

16. How has Sungrow achieved deep market penetration in the US, especially regarding assembly, maintenance, and after-sales personnel?

Sungrow's successful market penetration in the US is mainly due to two factors. First, after Huawei was suppressed and withdrew from the market, Sungrow absorbed a large number of experienced Chinese-American professionals who previously worked at Huawei, quickly establishing its channel, technical, and after-sales service systems and enhancing brand recognition.

Second, Sungrow's talent development is gradual. Unlike companies such as LG Chem, which attempt to dispatch a large number of engineers at once, Sungrow has gradually cultivated and accumulated talent in the US market over nearly a decade. This gradual localization strategy is fundamentally different from large-scale short-term personnel deployment and is more stable and effective. In addition, Sungrow is actively expanding global production capacity, such as planning 10 GWh of PACK and 5 GWh of battery cell capacity in Egypt.

17. In terms of pricing strategy for system integration products between data center and traditional grid customers, what are the differences between Sungrow and Fluence?

Fluence's product pricing is generally higher than Sungrow's due to its higher-end customer base. Specifically, Sungrow's project quotations for data center customers (such as CSPs) are about 15% higher than those for traditional power generators (such as NextEra). This is determined by the current supply-demand relationship, as only Chinese companies can quickly meet the specific needs of CSPs. In contrast, Fluence reportedly offers the same prices to its data center and grid customers, without additional markups.
