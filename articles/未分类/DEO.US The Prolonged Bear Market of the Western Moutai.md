Diageo (DEO.US) was once the world's largest listed spirits company by market capitalization. Although headquartered in London, its largest market is the US (accounting for over one-third of revenue), and its business now spans 180 countries worldwide. Over a decade ago, a prominent domestic private equity tycoon often referred to Moutai as the "Chinese version of Diageo," until DEO was surpassed by Moutai in market cap in 2017 and in revenue in 2024—now, Moutai and DEO have entered their fifth and fourth consecutive years of bear markets, respectively. The aforementioned private equity tycoon almost completely liquidated his A-share and Hong Kong stock holdings in 2022, went all-in on US stocks starting in 2023, and has never mentioned Moutai again.

![](https://image.acecamptech.com/article_image/50517705/0.19377629235641713.png)

In February this year, DEO released its FY2026H1 results, showing declines in both price and volume, and its H2 outlook also indicated continued deterioration, which was extremely poor; at the same time, DEO announced a 50% dividend cut, breaking the long-standing expectation of stable dividends, leading to a sharp share price drop starting at the end of February.

Perhaps many have already guessed that its China revenue declined sharply due to the real estate bear market. Shui Jing Fang (63% owned by DEO) saw its results collapse: net profit for H1 2025 was -56.5%; for FY2026 H1, Greater China organic sales were -42.3%, and baijiu volume was -50.4%. Growth for imported spirits such as Johnnie Walker and Singleton in China has stagnated, with channel inventory remaining high.

However, few would have expected that DEO's US revenue also declined significantly. This is quite interesting, as US housing prices have not experienced a bear market.

![](https://image.acecamptech.com/article_image/50517705/0.42142385110569436.png)

![](https://image.acecamptech.com/article_image/50517705/0.7063316073644754.png)

![](https://image.acecamptech.com/article_image/50517705/0.3645762941735423.png)

![](https://image.acecamptech.com/article_image/50517705/0.38135967631174017.png)

Overall, DEO has maintained stable market share in the spirits sector; while some individual brands may have seen increases or decreases, the overall market share has not changed significantly—therefore, its prolonged bear market is not due to a deteriorating competitive landscape.

![](https://image.acecamptech.com/article_image/50517705/0.7296273094264976.png)

![](https://image.acecamptech.com/article_image/50517705/0.6060948241410095.png)

According to DEO's own analysis, the primary factor behind its major bear market is inflation, as consumers in the UK and US have been forced to cut discretionary spending; the Iran war may further exacerbate the situation. Then, other major factors listed by DEO include GLP-1 weight-loss drugs—this is precisely the main reason for writing this article.

NVO and LLY's original GLP-1 weight-loss drugs have mainly been sold in Europe and the US in recent years due to patent protection and have not yet been widely adopted globally. However, Novo Nordisk's semaglutide patent expired in India this week, and more than 40 Indian pharmaceutical companies and over 50 generic versions are set to launch. Similarly, six GLP-1 brands have already been launched in China, with pipelines from over 80 pharmaceutical companies under development—yet, it seems that few domestic investors view GLP-1 as a potential negative for Moutai.

![](https://image.acecamptech.com/article_image/50517705/0.9509750593078333.png)

![](https://image.acecamptech.com/article_image/50517705/0.6146787454002794.png)

According to financial media reports, DEO is considering selling its China business—coincidentally, DEO's main global competitor Pernod Ricard is also suffering heavy losses: in 2025, net sales in China fell by as much as 28%, mainly due to a tightening regulatory environment impacting premium on-premise channels, persistently weak macroeconomic conditions and consumer confidence, and elevated inventory levels in some channels suppressing sell-through. Meanwhile, financial reports show that Martell's organic net sales declined by 17%; however, excluding the China market, its sales grew by about 20%. Reportedly, 80% of Pernod Ricard China's revenue comes from Martell. The decline in Martell's sales in China is not only the main reason for Pernod Ricard China's sharp performance drop, but also directly drags down the brand's global performance, becoming a key constraint on its development in China.

![](https://image.acecamptech.com/article_image/50517705/0.5175489048380816.png)

DEO has taken the lead over Moutai on the path of revenue decline, but its ROE remains at a high level. With the widespread launch of generic GLP-1 drugs in China, whether Moutai's performance and valuation will converge again with DEO's levels remains to be seen.
