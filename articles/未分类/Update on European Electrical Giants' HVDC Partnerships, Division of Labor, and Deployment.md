1. Could you introduce SCHNEIDER ELEC's partnership strategy in the HVDC sector?

SCHNEIDER ELEC's collaborations with partners are structured at various levels, including strategic partnerships, co-development, and ODM or OEM models. Regarding the HVDC sector, SCHNEIDER ELEC has conducted long-term analysis of the Chinese market. HVDC has been developing in China for about a decade, progressing through stages such as 240V and 336V. As a company with a longstanding UPS business, SCHNEIDER ELEC analyzed the potential for HVDC to take off in China and whether its solutions would expand to core overseas markets like North America and Europe, thereby impacting its existing UPS business. The analysis concluded that, in terms of efficiency, HVDC offered no significant advantage over the company's UPS products, which could already achieve 97% or even 99% efficiency.

In terms of pricing, HVDC does have a cost advantage, but the market is mainly dominated by large buyers such as BAT, resulting in weak pricing power and a relatively niche market. Moreover, aggressively promoting HVDC products could create internal competition with the company's existing AC UPS business, leading to a scenario of 'fighting with one's own hands.' Therefore, SCHNEIDER ELEC did not directly enter this market in the early stages.

Although SCHNEIDER ELEC does not manufacture HVDC products itself, to meet the needs of certain customers (such as supercomputing projects), it procures equipment from suppliers and integrates its own medium-voltage transformers, low-voltage equipment, and other products to provide comprehensive solutions.

Regarding supplier tiers, the first tier is generally considered to include Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric, Delta, and Vertiv, while Dynapower and others are in the second tier.

With Alibaba launching the Panama architecture and engaging in joint development with Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric, the two parties have established a close partnership.

2. What was the background of SCHNEIDER ELEC's senior management visit to Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric in January 2026?

The visit by SCHNEIDER ELEC's senior management to Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric in January 2026 was driven by several factors: First, SCHNEIDER ELEC holds a national sales conference annually, inviting the CEO and other senior executives, and arranges visits to key clients and partners. The 2026 conference was held in Hangzhou, facilitating a visit to Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric, which is located in the same city.

Second, the data center business has become a key growth driver for the group, with its share rising from below 15% a few years ago to nearly 20% in the past two years. As a result, selecting data center partners for senior-level visits has become a strategic priority. Lastly, considering the historical cooperation with Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric and the industry trend of evolving from traditional HVDC to 800V HVDC solutions, SCHNEIDER ELEC ultimately chose Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric as a key future partner for senior management engagement.

3. How will the future cooperation model and technical architecture between the two parties be determined?

This senior-level meeting primarily served to express the intention and attitude toward cooperation at a high level. Specific details, including the technical architecture of HVDC solutions and business cooperation models, are planned for in-depth discussion in the first half of 2026. Due to the Spring Festival holiday, related work has only just commenced after the holiday. The focus of discussions will be to determine several key technical architectures for future cooperation and the corresponding business models.

4. When jointly defining product architecture with partners and end customers, who holds the leading role?

Product architecture is not determined unilaterally by any party, but mainly depends on end-user needs and preferences. Different customers, such as BAT or the four major CSPs in North America, have varying technical architectures. SCHNEIDER ELEC adjusts its solutions according to users' architectural preferences. For example, at different times, the closeness of SCHNEIDER ELEC's cooperation with AWS or Meta may vary, and the cooperation model is adjusted accordingly.

To address the needs of North American CSP customers, SCHNEIDER ELEC has a dedicated business unit equipped with complete teams for business, architecture, and strategy, maintaining regular meetings with clients and partners such as Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric. For instance, since 2024, SCHNEIDER ELEC and NVIDIA have held meetings roughly every two weeks to discuss future directions in power distribution and liquid cooling technologies, aiming to influence the technical roadmap. In addition, SCHNEIDER ELEC's R&D center in the northwest of Boston, North America, houses joint development labs with clients such as Google and Meta, where joint development and prototype testing are conducted in dedicated, access-controlled areas.

Despite the diversity of customer requirements, SCHNEIDER ELEC recognizes that full customization leads to inefficiency. Therefore, the strategy is to develop standardized solutions that cover 80% of basic user needs, with minor modifications and customization for unique requirements. For example, the Sidecar 800V edge cabinet solution launched in North America at the end of 2025 exemplifies such a standardized approach.

5. What is the positioning of SCHNEIDER ELEC's Sidecar 800V edge cabinet solution in the future product roadmap?

The Sidecar 800V edge cabinet solution is considered a transitional solution. According to NVIDIA's technology roadmap, from 2025 to 2027, its chip architecture will evolve from Blackwell to Rubin and Rubin Ultra, with single-cabinet power consumption increasing significantly—from 120–140 kW in the past to about 600 kW for Rubin Ultra in 2026, and potentially reaching 1 MW in 2027 or beyond.

SCHNEIDER ELEC's Sidecar edge cabinet solution has a capacity of about 1 MW, supporting two Rubin Ultra cabinets. However, a configuration where one power distribution cabinet serves only two IT cabinets is considered to have average cost-effectiveness. Thus, this is seen as a stage in an ongoing evolution. The overall technical architecture is evolving from traditional 48V HVDC to 56V, 48V, and now to the current edge cabinet solution. The future trend is for HVDC technology to gradually penetrate upstream from the IT side (RT side) to UPS and even the medium-voltage side, potentially evolving toward architectures like Panama.

6. Why did SCHNEIDER ELEC choose to strengthen its HVDC business through partnerships with external partners?

This is a strategic decision made after years of discussion and deliberation. SCHNEIDER ELEC holds an absolute advantage in AC power distribution on the IT side, and customers generally believe that if SCHNEIDER ELEC cannot deliver, other suppliers are unlikely to succeed. However, this also highlights SCHNEIDER ELEC's shortcomings in DC technology. In the context of rapid technological iteration, it would be too slow to build up power electronics capabilities from scratch internally. Therefore, partnering with external parties or pursuing acquisitions is the most effective way to quickly address weaknesses and enhance competitiveness in the DC field.

7. Is SCHNEIDER ELEC's cooperation with Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric in the HVDC field exclusive? Will other partners be introduced in the future?

In the early stages of new technology cooperation, SCHNEIDER ELEC prefers exclusive partnerships. This is because engaging with multiple companies simultaneously may cause partners (such as Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric) to question the level of commitment and the market share they can ultimately secure, making it difficult to achieve wholehearted engagement and deep alignment. This strategy also applies to partners, who face multiple choices, such as Vertiv or Eaton, so exclusivity helps establish a close relationship.

Looking back at SCHNEIDER ELEC's past experience, for example, in the early stages of lithium battery business development, SCHNEIDER ELEC was deeply aligned with Samsung. As the North American business expanded, SCHNEIDER ELEC later introduced LG as a new partner to enhance bargaining power and reduce costs, and subsequently further cooperated with CATL and others.

Therefore, cooperation with Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric may be exclusive in the short term. However, in the long run, as the business reaches a certain scale, new partners may be introduced to reduce costs or enhance supply chain resilience.

8. How deep is the cooperation between SCHNEIDER ELEC and Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric in the HVDC field? What value does each party prioritize?

The cooperation between SCHNEIDER ELEC and Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric is very deep. SCHNEIDER ELEC values Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric's project experience, power module technology, and system integration capabilities in HVDC. For large multinational companies, system integration capabilities are relatively weak due to cross-departmental and cross-business unit collaboration. Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric values SCHNEIDER ELEC's global business capabilities and channels as a Fortune Global 500 company, which can help them overcome previous barriers to international expansion. In the short term, this system-level cooperation is exclusive, but components such as DC circuit breakers will still be sourced from third-party suppliers.

9. Will SCHNEIDER ELEC consider forming a joint venture with Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric?

The likelihood of forming a joint venture is low. A few years ago, when developing the lithium battery business, SCHNEIDER ELEC considered forming a joint venture with companies such as CATL, hoping to change the company's nature to circumvent potential restrictions on foreign enterprises in the Chinese market. However, from a financial and execution perspective, this is very challenging.

For a listed company like SCHNEIDER ELEC, financial operations are relatively complex, and the company prefers to conduct capital operations through acquisitions. As long as business cooperation proceeds smoothly, branding is less important, especially in overseas markets, and forming a joint venture is not essential.

10. Besides HVDC, does SCHNEIDER ELEC cooperate with domestic companies in other data center infrastructure product lines such as UPS? What is the specific cooperation model?

Yes, for example, in the UPS product line, SCHNEIDER ELEC does cooperate with domestic companies. The R&D center for SCHNEIDER ELEC's UPS products was originally in Denmark, but development in Europe was slow, so much of the development work was later transferred to Shanghai. For large enterprises, the internal approval process for developing new products or power modules is complex, requiring justification of market size, investment, and returns. Therefore, SCHNEIDER ELEC decided to supplement certain product lines through OEM or ODM models. For instance, some mid-range UPS series power modules are sourced from domestic suppliers, mainly KSTAR. To avoid giving users the impression of 'rebranding' and to maintain product pricing, the industrial design aspects such as appearance, enclosure, control display, and related software are handled by SCHNEIDER ELEC to ensure consistency with other SCHNEIDER ELEC products (such as row cabinets). The technical core, which is more challenging to develop and helps reduce costs, is outsourced.

11. When jointly providing HVDC solutions to overseas markets with partners such as Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric, what is the division of labor and delivery model?

Collaboration and delivery models are case-specific. As a large enterprise, SCHNEIDER ELEC tends to retain high-value segments in-house; excessive outsourcing of high-value segments makes it difficult to guarantee profitability. Therefore, in the North American market, SCHNEIDER ELEC may take the lead in more segments.

In regions such as Southeast Asia, the situation may differ. For example, when large cloud service providers build data centers in Malaysia or Singapore, their requirements may not be as stringent as in North America, so partners such as Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric may take on more work, including cabinets and racks. R&D collaboration is global, but deliveries in each region are determined by customer circumstances and project requirements. Sometimes, customers specify brands for key components such as DC circuit breakers, which SCHNEIDER ELEC must comply with; if not specified, SCHNEIDER ELEC can select based on preference or a shortlist.

12. Regarding the cooperation between SCHNEIDER ELEC and Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric, when is a clearer business volume forecast expected?

A relatively clear business volume forecast is expected in the second half of 2026. The first half of 2026 will focus mainly on discussing the business cooperation framework, and decision-making processes in foreign enterprises are usually slower than anticipated.

13. If HVDC products developed with Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric enter the North American market, what is the potential value and pricing level?

Pricing in the North American market is generally higher than in China. HVDC products are expected to sell in North America at approximately RMB 4.5–5 per watt. In contrast, domestic prices are much lower. It should be noted that when discussing pricing, the scope of calculation must be clarified—whether it refers only to the main unit or the entire system including batteries and other components, as unit prices can vary significantly depending on the scope.

14. In light of the growth in the North American data center market, what is SCHNEIDER ELEC's penetration strategy for HVDC solutions?

Historically, HVDC solutions had very low penetration in North America. In fact, before Alibaba launched the 'Panama' architecture, SCHNEIDER ELEC had already conducted long-term analysis for clients such as Google, including evaluating SST (Solid State Transformer) solutions. At the time, clients abandoned these solutions due to high costs and unresolved technical challenges with SST, especially in protection. Later, Alibaba adopted this approach and promoted it domestically under the name 'Panama.'

15. What is the current competitive landscape of the HVDC market in China?

In recent years, domestic internet giants have alternated between suppliers such as Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric and Delta in projects to introduce competition, resulting in intense price competition and significant pressure on suppliers.

With the advent of a new wave of 800V HVDC technology, all suppliers hope to seize the opportunity for breakthroughs. SCHNEIDER ELEC attaches great importance to this cooperation, with the CEO personally engaging, and substantive follow-up is expected.

16. As data center power supply technology evolves toward HVDC, what role do Chinese manufacturers play?

China is at the global forefront in the practical application of HVDC data center technology. As cabinet power density continues to rise, traditional UPS solutions can no longer meet demand, and NVIDIA's technical white papers have accelerated this trend. In the 800V HVDC field, in addition to Delta and Lite-On, Chinese companies such as Hangzhou Zhongheng Electric have also developed corresponding capabilities.

17. From a technology evolution perspective, what are the key drivers for increasing HVDC solution penetration?

The adoption pace of HVDC solutions by customers is closely tied to the technology roadmap of GPU manufacturers such as NVIDIA. When single-cabinet power is just over 100 kW, traditional AC distribution solutions (such as busways) are feasible, and customers are less willing to change. However, as technology evolves to NVIDIA's Rubin architecture and single-cabinet power rises to 600 kW, traditional busway solutions will no longer suffice. At that point, switching to 800V HVDC solutions will become inevitable. SCHNEIDER ELEC is preparing and promoting solutions in advance based on its judgment of future technology trends.

18. Besides the increase in GPU power density as the core driver, what other key factors are accelerating the evolution of data centers toward 800V HVDC architectures?

In addition to meeting the high power density demands of GPU clusters, several other key factors are accelerating the shift to 800V HVDC:

First is space and cost efficiency. HVDC solutions can significantly reduce the size of power supply equipment. For example, compared to traditional UPS solutions that require four or five cabinets, a 400V HVDC solution may need only one or two cabinets, and the reduction in components directly lowers costs. The medium-voltage rectifier or SST direct supply solution recommended by NVIDIA is the ultimate optimized form.

Second is raw material costs, especially copper prices. Since 2026, copper prices have risen sharply and are expected to continue rising. Traditional low-voltage power supply solutions require multiple thick copper cables, and in building large-scale computing clusters (such as 10,000-card clusters), the high cost of copper cables becomes a major consideration. Adopting 800V HVDC can significantly reduce copper usage, thereby lowering cable costs and improving power supply efficiency. ByteDance has piloted the 800V solution directly in China, also out of consideration for cost control in future large-scale cluster construction.

Finally, there are energy efficiency constraints, especially in the Chinese market, where data center construction is subject to strict PUE requirements. Adopting more efficient HVDC power supply solutions helps meet regulatory requirements.
