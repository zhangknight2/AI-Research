Below are expert opinions:

**1. What are Huawei Ascend’s shipment targets and product mix for 2026? What is the mass production and customer adoption status of the Ascend 950 chip?**

Huawei Ascend’s plan for this year is 700,000 to 800,000 units. Of these, the confirmed supply of 910c inventory is less than 400,000 units, with the remaining volume mainly depending on the ramp-up of the newly launched 950 chip, which is initially planned to ship over 200,000 units. The Ascend 950 has begun small-batch shipments to all customers, but the final ramp-up is still uncertain. It is expected that leading internet customers will account for about half of Ascend 950’s shipments, but since Alibaba’s Pingtouge product is similar to the 950, its procurement volume will be relatively low.

**2. What are ByteDance’s procurement plans and application strategies for Ascend 950 and subsequent Cambricon products (such as 690)?**

ByteDance’s overall demand for Ascend 950 is expected to be in the hundreds of thousands, but the specific procurement volume and delivery schedule will depend on the 950’s production capacity and testing results. In terms of application strategy, Ascend 950 will mainly be used for inference and training of ByteDance’s LLMs (such as Doubao). Cambricon’s chips, including the already procured 580 and the upcoming 690, will primarily be used for its Search, Recommendation, and Advertising (SRA) business.

**3. Does the Ascend 950 chip currently support training tasks? What is ByteDance’s chip selection strategy for different scenarios such as AI inference and SRA?**

The PR version of Ascend 950 can perform training, but the results are average. It is expected that by October 2026, the DT version will officially support training. In ByteDance’s selection strategy, AI inference mainly uses Ascend cards, NVIDIA’s H20/L20, AMD cards, and its self-developed chips.

For SRA business, Cambricon’s chips are mainly used for training. Future Cambricon products will continue to focus on SRA training needs. Since ByteDance has already optimized other chips for legacy business scenarios and incremental demand is stable, Cambricon will need to find new business scenarios to enter the inference field.

**4. In AI inference businesses such as Doubao and SeeDance, how does ByteDance evaluate different AI chips? Which suppliers will provide incremental computing power?**

ByteDance selects AI chips based on cost-effectiveness, consistency, and compatibility. For Doubao’s computing power growth, it may be supported by ByteDance’s self-developed chips in the future, which are specially optimized for Doubao applications.

For SeeDance, AMD chips are currently the main choice, with Huawei’s 910C accounting for only a small share. Whether SeeDance will use the 950 or other vendors’ chips in the future is still under evaluation. Huawei’s 950 chip has just started ramping up, so it currently plays a supplementary role in ByteDance’s AI business and will not become a major supplier in the short term.

**5. From a business priority perspective, what is ByteDance’s core business in 2026? Will future AI application computing power growth mainly come from Doubao or SeeDance?**

In 2026, ByteDance will prioritize the SeeDance application as its top business. Nevertheless, it is still unclear which chip will be adopted to support this business’s development.

**6. What are Alibaba’s core inference-side applications in 2026? What is the composition of its computing power, and what is its attitude and procurement plan for domestic chips?**

Alibaba’s main inference-side application this year is the Qianwen model. In terms of computing power composition, self-developed PPU accounts for about 42–43%, with the rest mainly provided by NVIDIA and AMD chips. Alibaba has started evaluating and procuring domestic chips. It has already placed orders for Cambricon’s 590 chip. Hygon and Cambricon are considered domestic suppliers, but Hygon is still in the testing phase and has not yet achieved large-scale procurement.

**7. What is Cambricon’s overall shipment expectation for 2026?**

Cambricon’s total shipments in 2026 are expected to be between 700,000 and 800,000 units. ByteDance is the largest and fastest-growing customer, while other customers have relatively small procurement volumes and are still in the early stages. In addition, Cambricon has some targeted bidding projects for government and telecom operators.

**8. What are Hygon’s shipment targets, order status, and main customer composition for DCU products this year? What challenges does it face in expanding into the internet giant market?**

Hygon plans to ship 150,000 DCU units this year, but current orders are only in the tens of thousands. Its customers include internet companies, telecom operators, and government, but there is no absolute key customer, with main orders currently coming from government projects. Hygon’s penetration in the internet industry is very limited, with procurement volumes at internet companies only in the thousands. Customer testing feedback indicates poor stability and average compatibility, which are major reasons why Hygon has not achieved large-scale adoption at ByteDance, Alibaba, Tencent, and others.

**9. What is Hygon DCU’s future development strategy and market positioning? Why is its product performing weakly in the current market with strong AI inference demand?**

Hygon currently does not have clear potential growth opportunities, with most potential business coming from government projects. Hygon is considering spinning off the DCU business to avoid impacting its CPU business. In the competitive landscape, Hygon is not in the same tier as Cambricon and Ascend, and is considered a second-tier vendor with average performance. Its main advantage lies in bundling with CPUs, which provides some cost-effectiveness.

**10. For which core applications are Tencent’s AI computing power purchases mainly used?**

Tencent’s AI computing power procurement is diversified this year. Tencent has also purchased chips from Kunlunxin. These computing resources are mainly used to support Tencent Meeting, WeChat, gaming, and other small-scale internal application scenarios.

**11. What is the current status of Tencent’s self-developed Hunyuan LLM? If the model is marginalized, what alternative strategies will Tencent adopt for AI product development?**

Tencent lacks confidence in the development of the Hunyuan LLM. If the model is marginalized, products such as Yuanbao and WeChat AI will increasingly adopt third-party open-source models in future development.

**12. What is the current supply-demand relationship in China’s AI chip market? What capacity challenges do self-developed and domestic chips face, respectively?**

Overall, demand for AI chips in China exceeds supply. The main supply-side bottleneck is the capacity ramp-up of advanced process nodes. Self-developed chips are highly dependent on overseas capacity, resulting in more pronounced supply volatility.

**13. To what extent do major domestic AI chip vendors rely on SMIC, Hua Hong, TSMC, and Samsung for foundry? What are the future trends?**

Early products from Ascend, Cambricon, Hygon, and others used TSMC for foundry, but once entering mass production, they all shifted to domestic supply chains, with SMIC as the main foundry. Vendors such as Enflame, Iluvatar CoreX, and Moore Threads are also transitioning to domestic supply chains. In contrast, Alibaba’s PPU still mainly relies on TSMC, though it is also evaluating a domestic production line, but no final decision has been made.

**14. What is the current market landscape for supernode products among domestic vendors? What is the market deployment, main customers, and supply issues for Huawei’s supernode products?**

Currently, Huawei is the only vendor with supernode products available for general sale. Other vendors such as Sugon, Baidu, and Alibaba mainly use their supernode products for government projects or internal use, without large-scale external promotion. Huawei’s supernode products have been widely deployed in telecom, financial, and internet industries, with main customers being telecom operators and government. Procurement from internet customers is limited, mainly due to delivery issues.
