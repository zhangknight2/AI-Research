1. What is the impact of Zimbabwe's restriction on lithium ore exports on the market?

The policy initially affected market sentiment, but the actual impact is expected to be limited. Zimbabwe has banned the export of lithium ore since 2022, and this policy adjustment mainly brings forward a measure originally scheduled for 2027. The motivation behind this move is that the export price of Zimbabwean lithium ore has long been lower than that of Australia, by about USD 1,000/ton as of early 2026. Therefore, the Zimbabwean government introduced this policy to retain the processing industry chain, tax revenue, and technology. The impact on the domestic Chinese market is short-term, expected to last one to two months. In the long term, this policy will not change the global lithium supply trend, as increased production from projects in Argentina, Chile, Australia, and other African countries can effectively fill the gap.

2. Which Chinese companies currently have lithium projects in Zimbabwe, and what is their progress?

There are mainly five companies with projects in Zimbabwe: Huayou Cobalt, China Nonferrous Mining, Shengxin Lithium Energy, Yahua Group, and Zijin Mining. Huayou Cobalt has relatively large resource reserves, about 50-60 million tons, with a planned annual output of around 400,000 tons of lithium concentrate. Its supporting 50,000-60,000 ton-scale sulfuric acid plant and 30,000 tons LCE project are expected to be commissioned in the coming months, with the 50,000 tons/year project already in trial production.

China Nonferrous Mining plans an annual output of about 600,000 tons of lithium concentrate, making it the largest lithium mine project in Africa. Its 10,000 tons/year lithium sulfate production line was completed in 2023. Shengxin Lithium Energy's Sabi Star project was commissioned in May 2023, with an annual output of about 290,000-300,000 tons of lithium concentrate, reportedly the highest-grade operating mine locally. Yahua Group also has a lithium concentrate project there. Compared with Huayou Cobalt, the projects of China Nonferrous Mining, Shengxin Lithium Energy, and Yahua Group are relatively behind schedule. The policy has little impact on Huayou Cobalt and China Nonferrous Mining, but may have a short-term impact on the lithium concentrate exports of Shengxin Lithium Energy and Yahua Group. However, the combined export volume of these two companies is about 5,000 tons per month. If approval is obtained after two months, the actual impact will be less than 10,000 tons.

3. What is the proportion of Zimbabwean lithium ore in China’s total imports?

Over 90% of Zimbabwe’s lithium resources are exported to China. Based on 2025 data, imports from Zimbabwe are about 1.2 million tons, accounting for around 10% of China’s total lithium ore imports, equivalent to about 120,000 tons LCE.

4. Has political turmoil and tightening policies in some African countries had a substantial impact on local lithium mining and production?

Currently, the conflicts in Nigeria and Mali have not had a substantial impact on mining activities in lithium production areas. The conflict zones do not overlap with mining areas, and tax revenues during military government periods rely to some extent on mining, so production will not be disrupted in the short term. The main risks are policy-related rather than military conflicts.

Many African countries are showing a trend of amending mining laws, strengthening government supervision, and increasing the shareholding and tax rates of foreign mining companies. For example, Mali implemented similar policies in 2023 and Ethiopia in 2024. This trend aims to redistribute profits, and in the future, non-compliant small enterprises may be gradually eliminated, but there has not yet been a significant impact on total output.

5. What is the current market price of lithium carbonate? Please review the price trend in Q1 2026.

Currently, lithium carbonate futures are priced at over RMB 140,000/ton, and industrial-grade lithium carbonate spot prices are around RMB 150,000/ton. Reviewing Q1 2026, lithium carbonate prices first rebounded and then fell back. The price increase was driven by overseas supply disruptions, such as Zimbabwe suspending lithium concentrate exports, and delays in resumption of production in Jiangxi due to mining license changes and compliance rectification. These factors pushed prices from RMB 120,000-130,000/ton up to RMB 180,000-190,000/ton, while total social inventory fell to a historical low below 100,000 tons.

The recent price decline is mainly due to demand-side factors, especially the weak performance of the passenger car market, with growth expected to decline by 10-13%, largely related to the phase-out of domestic purchase tax subsidies. In addition, geopolitical instability has also negatively affected export expectations and market sentiment.

6. What is the outlook for lithium carbonate prices in Q2 and the second half of 2026?

Prices are expected to fluctuate with a strong bias in Q2, possibly ranging between RMB 140,000-180,000/ton. I believe it is unlikely for prices to remain below RMB 140,000/ton in the long term. Price support factors include the possibility that the resumption of production in Jiangxi may fall short of expectations, and production schedules in the energy storage sector have been arranged until the end of Q2. The outlook for the second half of the year is more optimistic, with prices expected to continue rising.

Although the growth rate of the new energy vehicle market is expected to slow, the increase in battery capacity per vehicle and rising oil prices may still drive higher penetration rates, with power battery demand expected to maintain 10-20% growth. More importantly, energy storage market demand is strong. Driven by policies such as new energy power generation entering the market and peak-valley price arbitrage, the economics of domestic energy storage projects are emerging. Overseas markets, driven by high oil prices and computing power demand, have full order books, with leading companies planning nearly 60% growth in energy storage shipments.

The global lithium market in 2026 is expected to shift from a slight surplus in 2025 to a tight balance, and monthly supply-demand mismatches may occur in the second half due to increased energy storage installations. Pricing logic may also shift from cash cost pricing to a model of full cost plus reasonable profit. Overall, the price range for the whole year of 2026 is expected to be RMB 120,000-200,000/ton.

7. What are the forecasts for global and Chinese lithium carbonate supply and demand in 2026?

Forecasts vary among institutions. One forecast suggests that global lithium supply in 2026 will be about 2.09-2.10 million tons, with demand at about 2.02 million tons, resulting in a slight surplus of about 70,000-80,000 tons. Another, more optimistic forecast is that global demand could reach 2.10-2.20 million tons, up 31% YoY, while global supply is about 2.22-2.23 million tons, with a surplus of only 30,000-40,000 tons, basically in tight balance.

For the Chinese market, domestic output in 2026 is expected to approach 1.4 million tons, plus about 300,000 tons of imports, for a total supply of about 1.67-1.68 million tons. Demand is expected to be around 1.7 million tons, also showing a tight balance.

8. What is the current price level of lithium concentrate, and what is the price outlook?

Prices have declined recently. The CIF price of 6% spodumene concentrate from Australia to China is around USD 2,000/ton, fluctuating between USD 1,970-2,100/ton. The domestic price of mica concentrate is about RMB 2,000/ton.

Looking ahead, lithium concentrate prices are expected to fluctuate in line with lithium carbonate prices, possibly stabilizing in the short term and trending upward in Q2 and Q3. Given the overall supply-demand pattern shifting from surplus to tight balance, prices are expected to remain strong throughout the year.

9. What is the status of resumption and production at Australian lithium mines?

In Australia, two mines have recently announced resumption of production. Core Lithium’s Northern Territory project has passed the final investment decision and plans to restart, with full production expected to reach 1.3 million tons of lithium concentrate per year by 2028. Pilbara Minerals’ plant in Western Australia also plans to officially restart in July, which will affect 200,000 tons/year of lithium concentrate capacity.

10. For Australian lithium mines that have suspended production due to profitability, how long does it take to resume, and what are the steps involved?

Historically, Australian lithium mines that have experienced shutdown cycles typically require 1 to 2 years for full resumption. This cycle is relatively short, as these mines were still operating in 2021-2022. The resumption process involves several steps: first, clearing accumulated water, mud, and sand from open pits; second, replacing and repairing beneficiation equipment that has aged or rusted due to long-term idling, with some sections possibly needing reconstruction; third, repairing infrastructure such as roads, water supply, and power supply, with electrical equipment maintenance being particularly time-consuming; finally, completing tendering, contract signing, and government filings to obtain mining production plans and other administrative approvals. However, not all Australian mines have completely shut down; some have only reduced capacity or suspended production plans. These mines can resume capacity much faster, possibly within a few months. Currently, most Australian mines are still producing at a small scale, with only a few high-cost mines completely shut down.

11. What is the status of resumption at the Jianxiawo mining area in Yichun?

Domestically, market attention is focused on the Yichun region in Jiangxi. Since August-September 2025, the region has been undergoing mining license renewal, with progress continuously delayed. The Jianxiawo mining area, which has the largest output, has basically completed its license renewal procedures, but the resumption timeline has been postponed from Q1 to the currently expected June, and whether this can be achieved remains uncertain. The ongoing delay in resumption is related to compliance requirements for mine type conversion (from kaolin to lithium) and issues such as back payment of taxes and fees.

Once resumption at Jianxiawo is confirmed, it will have a short-term impact and cause volatility in lithium carbonate futures prices. The market has always been highly sensitive to such news, and past trends have often been influenced by related rumors. If resumption is officially announced in the future, futures prices may experience new fluctuations.

12. For a new lithium mine project, how long does it typically take from obtaining mining rights and completing feasibility studies to actual mining?

The development cycle for lithium mine projects varies by type, location, and scale. In the feasibility study stage, domestic companies (such as Beijing General Research Institute of Mining & Metallurgy, Nanchang Ruijin, Changsha Research Institute of Mining & Metallurgy, and Kunming Metallurgical Research Institute) usually complete studies within one year. For ultra-large and complex projects, it may take one to two years, but the reports are relatively rough and mainly for reference. In contrast, Western companies (such as AMC and SRK) typically take more than two years for feasibility studies, with more detailed content that can directly guide construction.

13. What are the development cycles for hard rock and brine lithium mines, and are there differences between domestic and overseas projects?

During the construction phase, the cycles for different types of lithium mine projects are as follows:

Domestic hard rock lithium mines: In Yichun, Jiangxi, lithium mica mines benefit from favorable construction conditions, and medium to large mines can generally be commissioned within two years. In Sichuan and Xinjiang, pegmatite-type spodumene mines have longer construction cycles due to geographical and climatic conditions, generally requiring three years or more.

Overseas hard rock lithium mines: Large and ultra-large open-pit lithium mines in Western Australia typically have construction cycles of three to four years, with even longer periods for larger projects. African lithium mines are relatively scattered in scale, with construction cycles generally around two years.

In summary, except for domestic brine projects, whether domestic hard rock lithium mines or overseas hard rock and brine lithium mines, a new project generally requires a construction cycle of three to four years from the decision to mine to final commissioning.

14. What is the current level of lithium salt processing fees?

In March 2026, the processing fee for spodumene is about RMB 20,000/ton. This fee fluctuates by about RMB 2,000/ton depending on the company, raw material grade, cooperation model, and changes in lithium carbonate prices, generally ranging from RMB 18,000 to 22,000/ton. When lithium carbonate prices fall, processing fees are also adjusted downward; however, when prices rise, the upward momentum for processing fees appears limited.

15. What are the current cost curves for different types of lithium producers?

Production costs vary by resource type and business model. The lowest costs are for brine-based lithium extraction companies, such as those with salt lake resources in Qinghai and Tibet, with costs as low as RMB 30,000-40,000/ton, offering a natural cost advantage. Next are companies with their own lithium mines, especially those using lithium mica as the main raw material, such as Tianqi Lithium and Ganfeng Lithium. Due to lower ore grades, their costs are relatively higher, around RMB 60,000-80,000/ton. The highest costs are for companies without their own mines, relying entirely on purchased or imported ore. If they also pay about RMB 20,000/ton in processing fees, their production costs may exceed RMB 100,000/ton.
