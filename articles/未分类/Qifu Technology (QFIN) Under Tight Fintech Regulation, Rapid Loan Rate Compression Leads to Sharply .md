## **I. 4Q25 Results and 1Q26 Outlook: Loan Origination and Profit Take Rate Hit the Lowest Levels Since 2021, APR Compression Is Resolute, New Loan Asset Quality Is Recovering**

- **Credit Balance:** RMB 126 billion, YoY -8%, QoQ -9%, marking three consecutive quarters of decline; loan origination YoY -22%, QoQ -16%, with quarterly origination at the lowest level since 2021.
- **Net Profit:** RMB 1.07 billion, YoY -46%, QoQ -29%; impacted by both loan rate compression and deteriorating quality of outstanding credit. Average APR fell 140bp QoQ to 19.5%, proactively meeting the consumer finance window guidance of an average 20% rate.
- **Net Profit Annualized Take Rate (based on average balance):** 3.3%, compared to 6.0% in the same period last year and 4.3% in the previous quarter; excluding the one-off impact of CB buyback, operating profit take rate was only 2.8% vs. 5.1% in the previous quarter and 7.4% in the same period last year. Apart from the pandemic period, this is a historical low. Management hopes to maintain around 3% after regulatory stabilization.
- **Asset Quality:** After tightening risk controls, the quality of new loans has improved significantly. 4Q25 new loan FPD30 dropped 18% QoQ, reaching a near two-year low in December. January FPD7 improved another 10% over December, also hitting a two-year best; February C-M2 has returned to July–August 2025 levels. However, outstanding loans still face short-term pressure, with on-balance-sheet loan provision coverage reaching 12.5% in 4Q25 (previous quarter: 8.9%), weighing on earnings.
- **1Q26 Guidance:** Non-GAAP net profit of RMB 900–950 million (YoY -51% to -53%), continued contraction in loan origination, improved customer quality, and a trend of further APR decline.
- **Buybacks and Shareholder Returns:** In 2025, total buybacks reached US$680 million, plus dividends of US$200 million ($1.54 per ADS), totaling 98% of annual net profit or 52% of current market cap. In 4Q25, QFIN prioritized discounted CB buybacks of US$460 million (for US$399 million in cash), generating RMB 270 million in gains, with about US$230 million in CBs still outstanding. **There are currently no new stock buyback plans for 2026, but management has clearly stated that the existing authorization has not been fully utilized (US$450 million remaining). Buybacks will resume opportunistically once regulatory and macro conditions stabilize. Management also plans to keep increasing DPS annually, with the current share price implying a dividend yield of over 11.5%.**

## **II. Fintech Regulatory Review: Latest Developments and Potential Paths**

**2.1.** **Implemented Interest Rate Cap Regulations**

- **New Lending Regulation ("Document No. 9")—24% Cap on Comprehensive Financing Cost:** The National Financial Regulatory Administration issued the "Notice on Strengthening the Management of Commercial Banks’ Internet Lending Business to Improve Financial Service Quality and Efficiency"—drafted in April 2025 and officially implemented on October 1, 2025. The comprehensive financing cost (including credit enhancement service fees) for commercial bank lending partnerships is capped at 24% IRR. Models such as "24+12 virtual rights" that circumvent the cap are prohibited. Lending platforms are subject to a head-office whitelist admission system.
- **Consumer Finance Company Average Comprehensive Rate ≤20% Window Guidance:** Local financial regulators have issued window guidance (informal, based on industry channel check) to consumer finance companies in their jurisdictions, communicated around October 28, 2025, and expected to be implemented in March 2026. The core requirement is that the comprehensive rate for new loans must not exceed 20%, and the average rate for outstanding business must meet the standard by the end of March 2026. Specific implementation varies by institution.
- **Online Microloan Companies—4x LPR Rate Cap:** The "Guidelines for Managing Comprehensive Financing Costs of Microloan Companies" were jointly issued by the PBOC and NFRA on December 19, 2025. By the end of 2026, the share of new loans with rates above 4x LPR (12%) must be significantly reduced; by the end of 2027, all new loans must have comprehensive financing costs within 4x LPR. For ultra-short-term loans (tenor ≤1 month), the cap remains 24%, but repeated rollovers to evade regulation are explicitly prohibited. The regulatory intent is likely to phase out microloan licenses as funding channels, concentrating funding sources in joint-stock and major city commercial banks.
- **Disclosure of Loan Costs:** On March 15, 2026, the "Regulations on Disclosure of Comprehensive Financing Costs for Personal Loans" were issued, requiring lenders to present a comprehensive cost disclosure form to borrowers, calculating the annualized comprehensive financing cost under normal repayment scenarios. No other interest or fees may be charged. Effective August 1, 2026.

**2.2. Pending Clarification, Not Yet Officially Documented (Based on Industry Channel Check)**

- **Consumer Finance Company Comprehensive Rate May Evolve from "Average ≤20%" to "Cap ≤20%",** with timing and scope yet to be determined.
- **City/Regional Commercial Bank Lending Scale Control:** The scale of lending/partnership loans is frozen at end-2025 levels, only allowed to decrease, not increase. For city and rural commercial banks with smaller lending businesses, internet lending must be cleared to zero by year-end.
- **Internet Banks—Some Assets Capped at ≤18% Comprehensive Rate:** Regulatory window guidance for private/internet banks engaged in online lending requires some asset structures to have rates ≤18%. The market is concerned this may be extended to other lending entities in the future.

**2.3. Scenario Analysis for Interest Rate Cap Regulation**

Regulators are currently clear in their policy direction to compress consumer finance and fintech lending rates (there was a similar regulatory push in 2021), especially in the context of an overall lower interest rate environment (1-year LPR has fallen from 3.85% in 2021 to 3.0%). This was analyzed in previous articles. **The core debate now is where the rate cap will be set, whether rate compression will become a long-term regulatory action, and whether different institutions will be treated differently.**

The overall regulatory intent is to squeeze the space for high-rate lending by regulating all licensed funding entities. The previous 24%-36% subprime products have been largely eliminated, with a 24% cap (as also required in 2021). Window guidance sets a 20% average rate cap for consumer finance companies, and further restricts lending scale at city/rural commercial banks, forcing a reduction in high-rate partnerships with small platforms. The 4x LPR cap for new microloans is intended to phase out internet microloans as lending entities, in line with the central government’s push to exit high-risk small financial institutions (forcing fintech platforms to shift to partnership models). **As for the rumored 18% cap for internet banks, whether it will be extended to other funding entities remains to be seen. The author believes this is unlikely to happen soon, as regulators are pushing rate compression under the premise of avoiding systemic risk. A rapid, across-the-board cut could lead to insufficient personal loan supply and credit risk spreading to the banking system (bank credit card loan quality is still deteriorating).**

By scenario:

**1. If the rate cap is only adjusted to 24%, average loan rates need only be reduced to 21–22%, simply abandoning previous ICE high-rate lead generation business. Listed platforms have already adjusted in 4Q25. However, current indications are that rate compression requirements will likely be stricter than in 2021.**

**2. If the average rate cap for funding entities such as consumer finance companies is set at 20%, QFIN has already adjusted in 4Q25 (IRR 19.5%), while FINV and LX remain high at around 22%. In this scenario, loans with rates between 20–24% are still allowed, but funding supply will be more constrained.**

**3. If the rate cap for funding entities such as consumer finance companies is lowered to 20%, then loans priced at 20–24% will essentially be banned. Subprime platforms will need to lower pricing by another 3–4% from scenario 1, and an average IRR of 18% would mean the overall UE for the current subprime segment turns negative. Platforms will need to acquire higher-quality customers (i.e., compete for the 14–16% segment currently served by Jiebei, Weili Dai, etc.), which is extremely challenging. Among listed platforms, only QFIN may have a chance to transform.**

**4. In the extreme case where the rate cap is set at 18% in line with internet bank window guidance, subprime customer platform UE would be negative, making survival nearly impossible. The author considers this scenario unlikely.**

**From QFIN’s UE perspective, new loan UE has declined significantly. Referring to management’s 3% operating profit take rate guidance, under scenario 2, assuming the current strategy of improving customer quality, new customer average IRR at 19%, funding cost may rise due to the microloan 4x LPR cap, possibly increasing from below 3% to 3.5%. Assuming credit cost for new customers falls to 8% (previously 9–10%), and operating cost remains at 4.5%, the operating profit take rate can reach 3%. If loan balance declines another 10% from 2025 to around RMB 113 billion, with a 20% tax rate and some SBC, steady-state net profit would be around RMB 3 billion.**

Short-term profit uncertainty lies in asset quality, as the asset quality of existing customers may be dragged down by industry liquidity issues. Another factor is the balance between loan scale and customer acquisition cost. As the company abandons high-risk customers and actively acquires higher-quality customers, unit acquisition cost has already risen in 4Q, and loan balance has returned to early 2024 levels, with further declines possible in 2026.

Medium- to long-term uncertainty mainly lies in 1) the steady-state requirement for lending rate caps, and 2) the impact of microloan license rate limits on low-cost funding such as ABS.

**What is relatively certain is that every industry downturn cycle presents an opportunity for industry leaders to gain market share. After the 2021–22 rate compression and industry downturn (plus the pandemic), QFIN seized business opportunities in 2024–25, with absolute profit levels returning to or exceeding 2021 levels.**

## **III. Is Valuation Cheap Enough, and When Will the Inflection Point Be Visible?**

**3.1 Current Valuation Is Below Historical Extremes, and Sell-Side Net Profit Expectations Have Been Sufficiently Revised Down**

QFIN’s current PB valuation is below 0.5x, already surpassing the historical low (0.6x). Unrestricted cash and short-term investments account for 63% of market cap (87% including restricted cash). 2025 dividend plus buybacks total a 98% payout or 52% of current market cap. The 2025 dividend payout ratio is 22%, with significant room for increase. Management expects DPS to continue rising, and without additional buybacks, dividends exceeding $1.54 per ADS imply a dividend yield of over 11.5%.

**Based on the above calculations, in a neutral scenario where QFIN maintains a 19% IRR, 3% operating profit take rate, and slight loan balance decline, net profit would be around RMB 3 billion. If the dividend payout ratio rises to 45–50% or above, the target DPS can be achieved. In this scenario, QFIN’s ROE would fall to around 12%, still slightly higher than China Merchants Bank’s ROE of 11% (currently valued at 0.8–0.9x PB).**

**Compared to other listed fintech companies, QFIN has already proactively compressed IRR. Some sell-side analysts have revised QFIN’s 2026 net profit forecast down to RMB 3.4 billion, with a sufficiently pessimistic view on rates. The downside for further cuts is relatively limited compared to other higher-IRR fintech companies.**

**3.2 Next Observation Path: Regulatory Cycle Inflection Point -> Asset Quality Cycle Inflection Point -> Profit Cycle Inflection Point**

- **Regulatory Cycle:** Already near the bottom, with discussions underway on an 18% rate cap, which matches the current credit card overdraft rate (daily rate of 0.05%, or annualized 18.25%). The new microloan regulation capping rates at 4x LPR (12%) has also been issued.
- **Asset Quality Cycle:** Leading platforms have improved new loan asset quality by tightening risk controls and enhancing customer acquisition quality (though at the cost of higher acquisition costs and lower margin). However, credit risk for existing customers may continue to surface for 1–2 more quarters (since the ban on rates above 24% started in October 2025, and outstanding loan tenors are 8–12 months).
- **Profit Cycle Inflection Point:** Once the rate cap is clarified and asset quality stabilizes, leading platforms will see loan origination growth recover, indicating that business opportunities outweigh risks. Only then will profit forecasts have room for upward revision, which may take 2–3 quarters.

**Additionally, if QFIN resumes buybacks, it would be a highly positive signal for the share price, both fundamentally and in terms of fund flows.**

**If regulators eventually repeat the 2023–24 scenario and relax the lending rate cap again, it would be a significant positive for QFIN, with both profit and valuation experiencing a Davis Double Play (QFIN’s share price rose about 4x from the 2023 bottom).**

**3.3 Key Risks**

1) Further regulatory compression of the lending rate cap to 20% or below, or setting this as a long-term regulatory target; 2) Additional macroeconomic shocks leading to further deterioration in consumer loan asset quality; 3) Intensified competition from internet giants’ financial businesses targeting lower-tier customers, impacting QFIN; 4) Tightening of institutional funding, causing funding costs to rise above expectations; 5) Short-term earnings drag from new business initiatives such as overseas expansion.
